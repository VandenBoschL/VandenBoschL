I am a computational biologist and molecular/cellular biologist (PhD, University of Washington) focused on regulatory genomics, single-cell epigenomics, and machine learning approaches to decode non-coding genetic variation and human disease. At SCRI, I lead bioinformatics workflows supporting research on inborn errors of immunity and chronic inflammation, partnering across wet-lab and clinical teams and mentoring junior researchers in reproducible genomics analysis.

--

### 🔬 Research Focus & Technical Areas
* **Translational & Clinical Informatics:** Supporting functional genomics workflows for rare immune disorders and inherited diseases.
* **Single-Cell & Epigenomic Profiling:** Integrating snATAC-seq, bulk/single-cell transcriptomics, and related 'Omics analyses to map cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility and transcriptional regulation.
* **Regulatory & Non-Coding Genomics:** Predicting the functional impact of non-coding variants within *cis*-regulatory elements (CREs) and enhancers using machine learning (gapped *k*-mer SVMs, deep neural networks) and *in silico* saturation mutagenesis.

### 📄 Selected Publications & Research Outputs
* **Decoding cell-class specific roles of non-coding variants in human retina**  
  *Scientific Reports* (2025) — Trained 18 cell-class-specific gapped *k*-mer SVM models on human retinal snATAC-seq data (>90% AUROC) to generate Variant Impact Prediction (VIP) scores validated against massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs).
* **Programming human cell type-specific gene expression via an atlas of AI-designed enhancers**  
  *bioRxiv* (2025) — Collaborative study using deep neural networks trained on chromatin accessibility datasets to design and validate synthetic, cell-type-specific enhancers across human cell states and mammalian retina.
* **Machine Learning Prediction of Non-Coding Variant Impact in Human Retinal *cis*-Regulatory Elements**  
  *Translational Vision Science & Technology* (2022) — Applied ML models and *in silico* saturation mutagenesis across ~47k human retinal CREs to prioritize regulatory variants in inherited visual disorders.
* **Developmental changes in the accessible chromatin, transcriptome and Ascl1-binding correlate with the loss in Müller Glial regenerative potential**
  *Scientific Reports* (2020) - Integrated ATAC-seq, RNA-seq, and ChIP-seq across retinal progenitors and maturing Müller glia to show that loss of chromatin accessibility and bHLH motif availability at neurogenic loci restricts *Ascl1*-mediated neural reprogramming.
